# Research Questions

Questions collected from the group, gathered from the call thread for the
3 September 2026 session and the days following. Grouped by theme; closely
related questions sit together rather than being merged, so the different
framings stay visible.

## Definition and boundaries

- Is personhood a boundary object — and if so, does the existence of boundaries
  bring persons into being?
- Where are the boundary conditions of personhood, and how might Margaret Radin's
  *Property and Personhood* (1982) be updated for the present?
- What role does unknowability play in personhood? What do attempts to predict or
  pregenerate persons mean for their personhood?
- Are adversarial doubles and monsters a category crisis that produces a new type
  of person?

## Rights, autonomy, and responsibility

- What is the connection between personhood and rights, and what are the minimum
  rights a person must have as a consequence of personhood?
- What level of autonomy do persons have, and how does it differ from the autonomy
  of non-persons?
- What is the relationship between personhood and rights (autonomy, the positive
  ability to do things) versus personhood and moral responsibility (freedom from
  harm and interference, moral consideration from others)?
- How are concepts of personhood used to *strip* rights rather than extend them —
  fetal personhood being the obvious case?

## Law, precedent, and political pathways

- What are the legal and political pathways towards recognition of personhood?
- Why has personhood for rivers, trees, and animals remained a side show in common
  law? The history seems marginal, without much precedent that would crystallize
  personhood for nature or animals.
- Does the requirement that someone else bring a legal claim on behalf of these
  entities — for which legal standing has no intrinsic meaning — stretch personhood
  past the point of usefulness?
- How did tort law change the way corporations came to hold personhood, and what
  does that suggest for other candidates?

## Nature and non-human entities

- Could a hyperobject like climate have personhood?
- If a forest, a mountain, or a deer has personhood, how can we know what it
  communicates?
- Can humans, or a computer intelligence, act as agent for a natural entity treated
  as a person?
- Can a natural entity have conflicting interests within itself? (Addictive
  behaviours in humans might suggest yes.)

## Collectives

- Can a collective of intelligences be considered a person — a hive mind, a colony?
  What explorations of this already exist?
- Corporations already fit this description; what does that precedent imply for
  other collectives?
- What of collectives with cognition and intelligence but no apparent sentience,
  consciousness, or experience?

## Artificial intelligence

- Is legal personhood for AI agents presumptively desirable?
- Who would benefit from an AI personhood protocol?
- Can values emerge from repeated iteration against an objective or benchmark on the
  part of AI models, and would that constitute the volition — preferences and values
  animating intent — that one construction of personhood requires?

## Fictive personhood

- What are the overlaps between character and personhood?
- Can anything fully fictional become a person? If so, in what sense, and what does
  that mean for them?
